Reading Technology Adoption in a Fictional Research Team

1 Introduction

Research teams often receive more articles than they can read closely. This fictional example studies whether a source-linked reading aid changes how quickly a team can identify a paper's research question.

The proposed aid does not replace the original paper. It preserves each source passage and places a note beside it so that readers can verify every explanation against the text.

2 Method

We compare two fictional groups of readers. One group receives ordinary summaries, while the other receives an annotated reading copy that explains how each passage contributes to the argument.

The comparison focuses on comprehension rather than speed alone. A reader who finishes quickly but cannot identify the evidence supporting a conclusion has not completed the intended reading task.

3 Expected contribution

The example illustrates a design principle: a useful annotation connects local wording to the paper-level argument and makes its evidence boundary visible.
